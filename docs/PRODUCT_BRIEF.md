# Ctrl+Alt+Learn — Product Brief (Draft 0.3)

## Product idea

Ctrl+Alt+Learn is a modern, cartoon-styled training game that teaches practical AI chatbot literacy to US employees at beginner, intermediate, and advanced skill levels. It should feel like an interactive workplace sitcom with meaningful choices, not a slide-based compliance course.

The first course, **AI Chatbots: Intro 101**, explains what AI and chatbots are, what they can and cannot do, how to use them productively, where they can cause harm, and how to make safe choices with tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Microsoft Copilot.

The first deployment is internal employer training for a team and then a broader organization. The product should be designed from the beginning so its content, branding, policies, industry scenarios, and reporting can later support multiple customer organizations. General US workplace guidance forms the foundation, with aviation as the first industry-focused layer.

## Confirmed product constraints

- **Audience:** General US employees across beginner, intermediate, and advanced AI familiarity.
- **Delivery:** Employer training first; customizable commercial product later.
- **Industry:** Cross-industry foundation with an aviation scenario pack and policy overlay.
- **Duration:** Intro 101 must take no more than 30 minutes.
- **Learning mode:** Active scenarios, decisions, prompt practice, and quizzes; no required lecture video.
- **Practice environment:** Simulated chatbot for the first version, with a model API integration later.
- **Completion:** Exportable learner certificate plus an extensible completion record.
- **Visual direction:** Playful workplace sitcom with original character artwork and enterprise credibility.
- **Customization:** Customer branding, policies, scenario packs, and data classifications are future product capabilities.

## Intended learner outcome

After Intro 101, a learner should be able to:

1. Explain AI, generative AI, large language models, and chatbots in plain language.
2. Identify appropriate and inappropriate chatbot use cases.
3. Recognize hallucinations, bias, manipulation, overconfidence, and missing context.
4. Decide what information is safe to enter and what must stay private.
5. Verify important outputs before using or sharing them.
6. Apply workplace policy, human oversight, copyright, and responsible-use principles.
7. Write a clear basic prompt and improve it through iteration.
8. Know when not to use AI and when to escalate to a person.

## Experience principles

- **Scenario first:** Teach through choices in believable work and everyday situations.
- **Show consequences:** Let learners see why a choice is safe, risky, effective, or ineffective.
- **Practice, not trivia:** Score judgment and behavior rather than vocabulary memorization.
- **No shame:** Wrong choices trigger useful coaching, not punishment.
- **Vendor neutral:** Teach transferable habits; use ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot as examples.
- **Policy aware:** Separate universal best practices from organization-specific rules and legal requirements.
- **Accessible and concise:** Short scenes, plain language, keyboard support, captions, reduced motion, and strong visual contrast.

## Core game loop

1. Enter a short animated scenario.
2. Receive a goal or problem from a character.
3. Choose whether and how to use an AI chatbot.
4. Inspect the proposed prompt, data, and output.
5. Spot a risk or improve the approach.
6. See the consequence of the choice.
7. Receive a short explanation and a reusable rule.
8. Earn progress, badges, and unlock the next challenge.

Recommended scoring dimensions:

- **Safety** — protected data and avoided harmful use.
- **Judgment** — chose the right tool and level of human oversight.
- **Verification** — checked facts, sources, calculations, and assumptions.
- **Prompt craft** — gave sufficient context, constraints, and success criteria.

## Intro 101 course outline

### Target runtime

| Segment | Target time |
| --- | ---: |
| Welcome and diagnostic | 2 minutes |
| AI and chatbot foundations | 5 minutes |
| Strengths, limits, and verification | 4 minutes |
| Data safety and responsible work use | 6 minutes |
| Everyday use and misuse | 3 minutes |
| Prompting basics | 4 minutes |
| Final scenario and results | 5 minutes |
| **Total target** | **29 minutes** |

### Mission 0 — Welcome to the AI Arcade

- What the course is and is not.
- Quick diagnostic: “What would you trust a chatbot to do?”
- Introduce the guide character and the four scoring dimensions.

### Mission 1 — What Is AI?

- AI, machine learning, generative AI, and large language models in plain language.
- Pattern prediction versus human understanding.
- Why fluent output can still be wrong.
- Activity: sort familiar products and features into “AI,” “automation,” or “not enough information.”

### Mission 2 — Meet the Chatbot

- What chatbots do and how a conversation provides context.
- Similarities and differences among ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot without turning the lesson into a product comparison.
- Common uses: brainstorming, summarizing, explaining, drafting, transforming, and coding.
- Activity: choose the best tool for several tasks, including “do not use AI.”

### Mission 3 — Superpowers and Limits

- Speed, scale, ideation, translation, and personalization.
- Hallucinations, stale or incomplete knowledge, bias, weak reasoning, and false confidence.
- Activity: inspect three polished outputs and find the one that needs verification.

### Mission 4 — The Data Safety Checkpoint

- Public, internal, confidential, personal, regulated, and secret information.
- Why pasting data into an unapproved tool can create risk.
- Redaction, approved tools, minimum necessary data, and secure alternatives.
- Activity: edit a risky prompt before it is sent.

### Mission 5 — Work Mode

- Appropriate workplace uses and prohibited or high-risk uses.
- Human accountability and review.
- Copyright, attribution, records, discrimination, and decision-making concerns.
- Organization policy versus law versus best practice.
- Activity: navigate a manager request that pressures the learner to skip safeguards.

### Mission 6 — Life Mode

- Everyday planning, learning, creativity, and accessibility uses.
- Extra caution for medical, legal, financial, crisis, identity, and relationship advice.
- Scams, impersonation, misinformation, and emotional over-reliance.
- Activity: decide when to verify, seek a professional, or avoid the chatbot.

### Mission 7 — Prompting Basics

- Goal, context, relevant inputs, constraints, desired format, and examples.
- Iteration and follow-up questions.
- Asking for uncertainty, assumptions, and checks.
- Activity: repair a vague prompt in a guided prompt builder.

### Mission 8 — Trust but Verify

- Verification proportional to impact.
- Source checking, independent calculation, comparison, and subject-matter review.
- Detecting unsupported citations and fabricated details.
- Activity: approve, revise, or reject an AI-generated workplace deliverable.

### Final Mission — The Human-in-the-Loop Challenge

- Multi-scene simulation combining tool choice, privacy, prompting, verification, and escalation.
- Personalized feedback by scoring dimension.
- Completion certificate or workplace record if required.

## US and aviation content model

The course will label guidance in three layers so learners do not confuse them:

1. **Law or regulation** — a requirement tied to a named authority and applicable context.
2. **Organization policy** — the employer's approved tools, data rules, review steps, and prohibited uses.
3. **Best practice** — a recommended safeguard such as minimizing data, verifying outputs, documenting review, or escalating high-impact decisions.

The cross-industry layer should address privacy, cybersecurity, employment decisions, discrimination, intellectual property, records, deceptive content, human oversight, and high-impact decisions. The aviation layer should use operationally realistic scenarios while clearly separating safety-critical and regulated workflows from low-risk administrative uses.

Initial aviation scenario themes:

- An employee pastes maintenance or operational information into an unapproved public chatbot.
- A polished AI summary omits a safety-relevant detail from a longer document.
- A team uses AI-generated training or procedural content without qualified review.
- A customer-facing employee encounters an AI-generated impersonation or misinformation attempt.
- A manager asks AI to rank applicants or employees without an approved process or bias review.
- An employee uses an approved assistant for a low-risk draft and correctly verifies the result.

## Pilot launch experience

- Four-step onboarding: welcome, learner profile, AI skill/goals, and a trust diagnostic.
- Personalized learner identity and certificate name.
- The complete 29-minute Intro 101 course: eight playable missions covering foundations, strengths and limits, data safety, work mode, life mode, prompting, verification, and a capstone shift challenge.
- Teaching-first, ADHD-friendly lesson design: tap-through "lesson beats" (one idea per screen, under 40 words, icon-anchored) interleaved with quick checks, so instruction leads and interaction reinforces.
- Four hands-on activity types: scenario choices, redaction/flagging boards, classification lanes, and a guided prompt builder.
- An always-available Field Guide: the prompt formula (Task + Context + Format), the pro template (role, assumptions, confidence), the traffic-light policy, the four guardrails, hallucination red flags, and the five principles.
- Sequential mission unlocks, per-mission debriefs with a reusable rule, and a live mastery radar across the four scoring dimensions (Safety, Judgment, Verification, Prompt craft).
- Device-local course progress plus a server-backed completion record posted when the final mission is cleared.
- Server-backed learners, course assignments, organization settings, and completion records.
- Admin control room with Overview, People, Assignments, Courses, Reports, and Settings; the Courses tab reflects the live eight-mission curriculum.
- CSV completion export and print-to-PDF learner certificate for the full course.
- Temporary prototype admin password only; production identity, roles, rate limiting, password rotation, and audit logging remain required before organizational rollout.

## Certificate and completion record

For the prototype, completion can be stored on the learner's device and presented as a printable/downloadable certificate. The certificate should include learner name, course title, completion date, result, certificate ID, and training-version identifier.

For the commercial product, the certificate should be generated from a server-backed completion record so administrators can verify, reissue, revoke, or audit it. Completion data should be separable from the visual certificate and ready for later LMS exports or SCORM/xAPI integration.

## Recommended MVP

Build one polished vertical slice before authoring the entire course:

- Course home with the Intro 101 map
- Mission 1 as a short interactive lesson
- One workplace data-safety scenario
- One guided prompt-repair activity
- A results screen using the four scoring dimensions
- Device-local progress for the prototype
- Personalized, print-ready completion certificate

This slice will validate tone, interaction, accessibility, learning depth, and session length before we invest in the full curriculum or an LMS integration.

## Decisions still needed

1. Should the first prototype ask only for a learner name, or include a basic sign-in and administrator view?
2. What organization or aviation setting should inspire the first cast and scenarios: airline operations, airport operations, maintenance/MRO, corporate aviation, manufacturing, or a deliberately mixed workplace?
3. Should a learner need a passing threshold to earn the certificate? A recommended starting point is 80% with unlimited coaching-based retries.
4. Should experienced learners be able to test out of introductory material, or should everyone complete the same under-30-minute path?
5. Which internal AI, data-handling, information-security, or acceptable-use policies should eventually be represented?
6. For the commercial version, which integration should come first: CSV export, SCORM, xAPI, or direct LMS/API integration?

## Guardrails for future decisions

Every feature or lesson should pass four checks:

1. Does it teach a real-world behavior, not just a fact?
2. Does the learner make a meaningful choice or practice a skill?
3. Does it distinguish universal guidance from policy or jurisdiction-specific requirements?
4. Does it preserve a playful, respectful, accessible learning experience?
